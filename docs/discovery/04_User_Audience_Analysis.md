# 04 — User & Audience Analysis

This section is StateNext Labs' UX analysis, built on the organizational and audit research in `02` and `03`. Audience needs and barriers below are reasoned from SOC Alliance's actual, verified programs and current site behavior — not invented personas with fabricated quotes or statistics.

## Audience Profiles

### 1. Community Members / Residents (primary)
**Likely looking for:** what SOC Alliance actually does in their neighborhood, upcoming events, how to get involved locally.
**Key questions:** "Is this organization active near me?" "What's happening this month?" "Who runs this?"
**Desired action:** attend an event, sign up for a program, follow on social media.
**What should appear first:** clear evidence of current, local activity (not a nine-month-stale feed) and an easy way to see "what's near me."
**Barriers today:** the stale "Updates" feed makes the org look inactive; there is no way to filter events/programs by neighborhood or interest.

### 2. Students & Families Seeking Scholarships/Mentoring (primary)
**Likely looking for:** Talent Hunt eligibility and application details, scholarship amounts/deadlines, mentoring program details.
**Key questions:** "Am I eligible?" "How much is the scholarship?" "When is the deadline?" "Is this legitimate?"
**Desired action:** apply, or have a parent/guardian apply on their behalf.
**What should appear first:** clear, current eligibility criteria and deadlines, not a generic job-posting-style page.
**Barriers today:** the Talent Hunt has no dedicated evergreen page (only past-event recap posts); the Violence Prevention/mentoring page has almost no program description before the application link.

### 3. Individual Donors (primary)
**Likely looking for:** proof the organization is legitimate and effective, clarity on where money goes, an easy way to give once or monthly.
**Key questions:** "Is this tax-deductible?" "Is this a real, well-run organization?" "What does my gift actually fund?"
**Desired action:** complete a donation without hesitation or drop-off.
**What should appear first:** trust signals (501(c)(3)/EIN, financial transparency) directly alongside the giving CTA — not after a click to an off-site domain.
**Barriers today:** the off-site Donorbox handoff with no trust context, no stated impact-per-dollar, and no recurring-giving framing on SOC Alliance's own page (see `03_Current_Website_Audit.md`, Section 4).

### 4. Volunteers (secondary, currently underserved)
**Likely looking for:** concrete, scheduled ways to help (events, mentoring, tutoring, block-club involvement).
**Key questions:** "What can I actually do?" "How much time commitment is involved?" "Is there a background check for youth-facing roles?"
**Desired action:** sign up for a specific opportunity.
**What should appear first:** a short list of current, real volunteer opportunities — not an abstract mention of "volunteerism" under one pillar.
**Barriers today:** no dedicated volunteer page or sign-up form exists on the current site at all — this is a genuine content gap, not just a UX gap.

### 5. Businesses / Sponsors (secondary)
**Likely looking for:** sponsorship tiers and benefits (visible today only for Mardi Gras), general partnership opportunities, tax/marketing benefits of sponsoring.
**Key questions:** "What do I get for sponsoring?" "Who else sponsors this organization?"
**Desired action:** commit to a sponsorship or reach out to discuss partnership.
**What should appear first:** sponsorship tiers and what they fund, plus (once available) a partner-logo wall showing existing sponsors as social proof.
**Barriers today:** sponsorship information exists only in the context of one annual event, with no year-round corporate-giving page.

### 6. Community & Institutional Partners (secondary)
**Likely looking for:** confirmation of SOC Alliance's legitimacy, scope, and track record before entering a formal partnership (grant funders, government agencies, fellow nonprofits).
**Key questions:** "What is this organization's capacity and track record?" "Who leads it?" "Is it financially sound?"
**Desired action:** initiate contact for a partnership or grant conversation.
**What should appear first:** leadership credibility (real bios), financial transparency, and a clear record of past partnerships/grants.
**Barriers today:** no financial transparency, generic board photos, and no dedicated "Partners" page undermine exactly the credibility this audience is evaluating.

### 7. Program Participants in Crisis (primary, highest sensitivity)
**Likely looking for:** mentoring/life-coaching support for people affected by firearm violence; this audience may be reaching the site at a moment of real personal difficulty.
**Key questions:** "Is this safe and confidential?" "How do I actually reach someone?" "Do I qualify?"
**Desired action:** apply or make direct contact, with minimal friction and maximum trust.
**What should appear first:** clear, warm, non-bureaucratic language; a direct phone/contact option in addition to a form; confidentiality assurance.
**Barriers today:** this page (a) is missing from the sitemap, meaning it is harder to find via search than any other page, and (b) uses a generic external Google Form with no confidentiality or "what happens next" messaging — a real gap for a genuinely sensitive audience.

### 8. Job Seekers (SVPI roles) (secondary)
**Likely looking for:** role descriptions, compensation, and qualifications for Violence Interrupter / Case Manager / Field Manager / Victim Services Facilitator positions.
**Key questions:** "What does this job actually pay and require?" "Is this a real, funded position?"
**Desired action:** apply.
**What should appear first:** real job descriptions and compensation ranges — currently absent entirely.
**Barriers today:** the current Violence Prevention page has almost no role detail before the application link.

### 9. General Visitors / Press / Researchers (secondary)
**Likely looking for:** a quick, credible overview of who the organization is (mission, history, leadership, financials) for due-diligence or journalistic purposes.
**Key questions:** "Who runs this and how long has it existed?" "Is there a press kit or media contact?"
**Desired action:** find the information without contacting the org directly, or reach the right person quickly if they need to.
**What should appear first:** a real About/History page (currently missing) and clear leadership information.
**Barriers today:** no About page exists at all; grant-announcement blog posts are the closest thing to a press-facing resource, and they are hard to discover (poor URLs, not in a "Press" category).

## User Journey Maps

### Journey A — First-Time Individual Donor
1. **Discovery:** arrives via a social share, a search for "Woodlawn Chicago nonprofit," or a referral after hearing about the Mardi Gras Fundraiser or Giving Tuesday.
2. **Evaluation:** lands on the homepage, looking for quick proof the organization is real and effective — mission clarity, evidence of recent activity, and some indication of impact.
3. **Decision point:** clicks "Donate," expecting either an on-page giving experience or, at minimum, clear reassurance before being sent elsewhere.
4. **Current failure point:** is sent to an unbranded `donorbox.org` page with no preceding tax-deductibility or security messaging — the exact moment hesitation peaks.
5. **Redesigned experience:** the Donate page states 501(c)(3)/EIN status and tax-deductibility up front, offers a one-time/monthly toggle, ties gift amounts to concrete outcomes, and either embeds Donorbox on-page or clearly previews what happens on the next screen before the click.

### Journey B — Parent Researching the Talent Hunt Competition for Their Child
1. **Discovery:** hears about the Talent Hunt through a school, church, or the Sigma Omega Chapter network; searches for "SOC Alliance Talent Hunt."
2. **Evaluation:** wants eligibility rules, the application deadline, what's being judged, and scholarship amounts — ideally on one clear, current page.
3. **Current failure point:** only past-event recap blog posts exist; there is no evergreen "Talent Hunt" program page with next year's dates and rules.
4. **Decision point:** either finds enough information to help their child apply, or gives up and calls/emails instead — adding friction and staff workload.
5. **Redesigned experience:** a dedicated, evergreen Talent Hunt page under the Scholarship pillar with current-year eligibility, deadline, prize structure, and a simple application path, plus a "past winners" showcase for credibility.

### Journey C — Person Affected by Firearm Violence Seeking Mentoring Support
1. **Discovery:** referred by a case worker, community member, or found via a search that may not even reliably surface the page today (it's missing from the sitemap).
2. **Evaluation:** needs to quickly understand what the program offers, whether it's confidential, and how to reach a real person — this is likely a moment of real vulnerability, not casual browsing.
3. **Current failure point:** thin page content, a generic external Google Form, and a page that search engines may not even be indexing properly.
4. **Decision point:** either completes the external form or, if the page feels impersonal or hard to trust, does not follow through.
5. **Redesigned experience:** a warm, plain-language page (properly indexed) that explains what mentoring/life-coaching involves, states confidentiality clearly, offers both a form and a direct phone/text contact option, and sets expectations for what happens after someone reaches out.

### Journey D — Prospective Volunteer
1. **Discovery:** hears about SOC Alliance generally and wants to help, or is searching "volunteer Woodlawn Chicago."
2. **Evaluation:** looks for a "Volunteer" or "Get Involved" link in the main navigation.
3. **Current failure point:** no such page or link exists today — "volunteerism" is only mentioned as a sub-bullet under the Community pillar.
4. **Decision point:** without a clear next step, most visitors will not proactively email or call to ask "how can I volunteer?" — the opportunity is lost silently.
5. **Redesigned experience:** a dedicated "Get Involved" page listing specific, current volunteer opportunities (by pillar and by time commitment) with a simple sign-up form, positioned clearly in main navigation.
